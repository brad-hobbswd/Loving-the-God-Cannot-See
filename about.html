The writer behind the message

About the Author

Bradley Hobbs writes about faith that moves beyond words and becomes visible in the way we love people.

Bradley Hobbs

Bradley Hobbs is a Christian author, educator, and writer whose work explores the relationship between faith, spiritual growth, and the everyday choices that shape how we treat one another. His writing is rooted in a desire to bring biblical truth into honest conversations about real life, including forgiveness, church hurt, healing, compassion, and the responsibility believers have to love beyond familiar words.

With a background in education and technology, Bradley values clear communication, thoughtful learning, and resources that make meaningful ideas accessible. These interests come together in his writing through practical teaching, reflective questions, and an emphasis on allowing faith to shape both character and conduct.

Bradley believes that devotion to Jesus should be reflected in the way people respond to those around them. Love is not simply something to profess in worship or conversation. It is demonstrated through patience, mercy, humility, forgiveness, wisdom, and the willingness to see people with compassion without pretending that hurt does not exist.

His purpose in writing is not to offer easy answers or dismiss the complexity of painful experiences. It is to invite readers to examine their hearts, pursue genuine healing, and allow the Spirit of Jesus to make the love of God visible through their lives.

Bradley Hobbs

About This Book

Loving the God We Cannot See Through the People We Can See was written from the tension many believers experience between loving God and navigating difficult relationships with people. It considers how hurt, disappointment, guardedness, and unforgiveness can affect the way love is expressed.

Across six chapters, the book invites readers to consider how love for God becomes visible through the way they treat others. It does not ask readers to ignore harm, excuse wrongdoing, or abandon discernment. Instead, it calls them to pursue healing while allowing the character of Jesus to shape their responses.

The central message is both simple and challenging: the God we cannot see should be revealed through the love we show to the people we can.
